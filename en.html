Golden Visa Residency

According to the latest changes implemented in 2026, there are three options for obtaining the Greek Golden Visa through property purchase.

1‑ Golden Visa with a threshold of €800,000: In densely populated areas with high demand and populations above 3,100—such as Athens, Thessaloniki, and islands like Santorini and Mykonos—the minimum investment is €800,000 (with a minimum residential property size of 120 m²).

2‑ Golden Visa with a threshold of €400,000: In less densely populated areas and islands with fewer than 3,100 residents, the minimum investment is €400,000 (with a minimum residential property size of 120 m²).

3‑ Golden Visa with a threshold of €250,000: In all regions of Greece, if you purchase commercial, office, or industrial properties that have been converted into residential use, you can obtain the Greek Golden Visa with a minimum investment of €250,000 without any restrictions.

The Greek Golden Visa is granted to non‑EU nationals who invest €250,000 in real estate or other eligible investment routes in Greece. By obtaining the Greek Golden Visa, investors and their families (parents, spouse, children, and spouse’s parents) receive a renewable 5‑year residency permit. If the investor keeps ownership of the property for 7 years (the property is not sold), they may apply for Greek citizenship through naturalization, provided they reside in Greece for at least 183 days per year. Investors must also meet additional requirements, including proving integration into Greek society and passing a language exam. According to the CEFR framework, applicants must achieve at least level B1 in the Greek language exam, known as the Greek citizenship exam.

Investment opportunities: Residency in Greece allows applicants to benefit from investment and business opportunities in one of Europe’s growing economies. Greece’s strategic location and diverse markets offer many investment possibilities.

Lower cost of living: The cost of living in Greece is lower compared to many other European countries, allowing Golden Visa holders to enjoy a higher quality of life at more reasonable expenses.

Eligibility for citizenship: After 7 years of legal residency in Greece, Golden Visa holders may apply for Greek citizenship.

Stability and security: As an EU member state, Greece offers political and economic stability, ensuring a safe and secure environment for Golden Visa holders.

Greece Financially Independent Person (FIP) Residency:

The Greece Financially Independent Person residency, also known as the “self‑supporting visa,” is one of the simplest and most reliable ways to immigrate to Europe. It does not require a large investment, specific academic or professional background, or any language certificate. If you have stable income or significant savings, you can obtain Greek residency for yourself and your family without needing to invest heavily or start a business.

To obtain the Greek FIP residency, you only need to prove your financial ability through bank account balance or valid monthly income, allowing you to begin life in one of Europe’s most beautiful and historic countries. The FIP residency is ideal for individuals who can prove they have stable income sufficient to cover living expenses in Greece. It is a valuable opportunity for starting life in Europe and gaining Schengen residency. The Greek FIP residency (category I.8) is initially issued for 3 years and can be renewed every 3 years as long as the financial conditions are maintained.

With a Type D Schengen visa + opening a bank account in Greece, you will enter Greece to obtain the Financially Independent Person residency (I.8) and open a bank account. One of the most attractive benefits of this residency is that your bank account balance is not taxed. Additionally, consistently proving financial ability during renewals helps pave the way for permanent residency in Europe and facilitates obtaining a Greek passport.

Requirements for Greece Financially Independent Person Residency

To obtain Greek residency through financial independence in 2026, applicants must meet specific defined criteria. This residency type is based on the applicant’s financial ability and requires documents proving that they can cover living expenses in Greece without working or earning local income. Generally, the requirements include:

1. Financial Ability

The applicant must prove their financial capability. This means having sufficient bank account balance or stable income not dependent on employment. According to new regulations, the minimum required bank balance for the main applicant is €30,000.  For each dependent, additional funds must be added (for spouse €15,000, and for each child €7,000–€8,000).

2. Adequate Income

Since the Greek FIP residency does not grant permission to work in Greece, the applicant must prove that they have sufficient monthly income to live in Greece without working. This income may come from various sources such as bank deposit interest, rental income, or pension payments. The required amount for the main applicant is approximately €3,500 per month. It must also be proven that this €3,500 monthly income comes from sources outside Greece. For dependents, income requirements increase by 50% for the spouse and 15% for each child.

3. Physical Presence Requirement

To maintain the Greek FIP residency, applicants must spend at least 183 days (six months and one day) per year in Greece. This is the minimum physical presence required to keep the residency valid. Failure to meet this requirement may result in cancellation of residency and loss of its benefits. It is recommended to keep records of entry and exit dates to prove physical presence if needed. For short-term travel or initial entry into Greece before applying for FIP residency, applicants may use a Greek tourist visa.

4. Age Requirement

There is no specific age restriction for applying for the Greek FIP residency. Individuals of any age can apply for this type of residency—provided they are over 18 years old.

5. Health Insurance

Having valid health insurance is another requirement for the Greek FIP residency. The insurance must cover potential medical expenses in Greece, and the applicant must prove they have full coverage. Health insurance can be obtained from a private insurance company or the Greek public healthcare system.

6. Proof of Accommodation

The applicant must provide documents proving legal residence in Greece. These may include a rental agreement or property ownership documents showing that the applicant resides in Greece and intends to stay permanently.

7. Conditions for Family Members

Applicants may include their family members as dependents in the FIP residency application. According to Greek immigration laws, eligible dependents include the spouse and children under 18 years old. To include family members, the applicant must demonstrate sufficient financial ability to cover their living expenses. The applicant must also prove a legal marriage for the spouse to be recognized as part of the primary family unit.

Proof of Financial Ability for Living in Greece

To prove sufficient financial ability for obtaining the FIP residency, applicants must provide documents showing they can cover living expenses in Greece. Required documents include:

1. Bank Account Statement

A bank statement from the last 3 to 6 months showing at least €30,000 for the main applicant, €15,000 for the spouse, and €7,500 for each child. The account must be held in a reputable international bank or an approved domestic bank. The balance should not appear as a sudden deposit; financial transactions must show stable financial activity.

2. Proof of Stable Monthly Income

Applicants must provide documents proving at least €3,500 per month in non‑employment income.

New Regulations for Greek FIP Residency:

According to new Greek embassy regulations, the minimum required monthly income for the main applicant is €3,500. Additionally, this amount increases by 20% (€700) for the spouse and 15% (€525) for each child under 18.

Stage 2: Residency Application (I.8) in Greece  Main documents include a valid passport and Type D visa, proof of sufficient legal financial resources, private health insurance, and the €1,000 government fee receipt.
